Atomic Habits

James Clear · 2018 · Avery
320 pages 8 key ideas 11 min to read all ideas 4.8★ goodreads

The bestselling productivity book of the last decade. Clear's argument is simple and well-evidenced: small repeated actions, governed by good systems and aligned with the identity you want to inhabit, compound into outsized results. The reason you've heard the ideas before is that he didn't invent them — he packaged them better than anyone else.

The book in one line

You don't change by setting goals — you change by designing the smallest possible system that nudges you, every day, toward the kind of person who already does the thing.

8 ideas · curated by the editors · ~11 min read

The key ideas

In the order they're argued
01
Habits are the compound interest of self-improvement. 1% better every day is 37× better in a year; 1% worse every day, near zero.
Ch. 1 · 2 min
02
You don't rise to the level of your goals; you fall to the level of your systems. Goals set direction; systems produce results.
Ch. 1 · 1 min
03
Identity-based change beats outcome-based change. Don't ask "how do I run more?" — ask "what would a runner do?" and do that.
Ch. 2 · 2 min
04
Every habit follows the loop: cue → craving → response → reward. If any link breaks, the habit doesn't form.
Ch. 3 · 1 min
05
The four laws of behavior change: make it obvious, make it attractive, make it easy, make it satisfying. To break a habit, invert each.
Ch. 4 · 2 min
06
Environment beats willpower. The simplest behavior change is to change what's within reach.
Ch. 6 · 1 min
07
The two-minute rule: scale any new habit down until it takes less than two minutes to start. The hard part isn't doing it — it's starting.
Ch. 13 · 1 min
08
Never miss twice. One slip is an accident; two is the start of a pattern.
Ch. 16 · 1 min
